# Lane surface — the shot, held fixed

The reusable half of every track-surface prompt. Only `{{SURFACE}}` changes.

## Why this file exists

Two rounds of concepts were generated by describing the *object* and saying
nothing about the *shot*. Every one came back as a product photograph: the piece
floating on a grey studio background, in perspective, with a drop shadow. All of
them were unusable as a lane texture, and none of them were wrong about the
object — the prompts simply never said how it should be photographed.

Framing is not a detail you add at the end. It is the half of the prompt that
decides whether the output can be used at all.

## The fixed half

```
FRAMING — obey exactly:
Extreme wide letterbox crop. The surface FILLS THE ENTIRE FRAME edge to edge
with no border, no margin, no background visible anywhere. Flat orthographic
front view, camera perpendicular to the surface, zero perspective, zero
vanishing point. This is a flat texture swatch, not a photograph of an object.
No drop shadow, no studio backdrop, no table, no floor, no scene around it.
Nothing is cropped off at an angle; the surface edges align with the frame edges.

PRINTING:
Only three or four ink colours the way tin lithography worked, colour plates
very slightly out of register against the black line work, halftone dot screens
standing in for shading, flat matte litho ink over a faint metal sheen.

FORBIDDEN:
No characters, robots, toys, cars, animals, hands, faces, text, letters,
numbers, logos, watermarks, borders or frames.
```

## The variable half

One sentence naming what is printed on the surface, and where the running strip
sits. Keep the running strip explicit — the racers stand on it, and if the
prompt does not reserve space the generator fills the whole width with pattern.

## Budget

`nano-banana` at 3 credits is the draft tier — use it for concept sweeps of 6-8.
`nano-banana-pro` at 9 is the production tier, used once the concept is chosen.
`--dry-run` is free. `--budget` has no default and is required.

## Method note

Hold everything fixed except one variable, generate 6-8, and look at them side
by side. Reasoning about which single concept is right has been wrong three
times running; a sweep costs 24 credits and answers it by looking.
